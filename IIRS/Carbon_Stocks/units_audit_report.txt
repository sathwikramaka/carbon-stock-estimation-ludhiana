SoilGrids reports soil organic carbon in dg/kg, which is TEN TIMES the
value in g/kg. The carbon formula assumes g/kg. Punjab cropland typically
holds 0.2-0.6% organic carbon, which is 2 to 6 g/kg.

==========================================================================
  4. NET PRIMARY PRODUCTIVITY
==========================================================================
MODIS NPP (MOD17A3) is reported in kgC/m2/yr, and is often rescaled to
gC/m2/yr. Irrigated double-cropped Punjab reaches roughly 600-1200
gC/m2/yr. Note that NPP is ALREADY carbon, so multiplying by the 0.47
biomass-to-carbon fraction counts the conversion twice.

Agricultur

   expected   600 to 1200 gC/m2/yr
   VERDICT    consistent with the expected range
   note       values near 1.0 would indicate kgC/m2/yr

==========================================================================
  WHAT TO DO
==========================================================================
1. Open the Google Earth Engine script that produced these exports and
   check the units documented for each band. SoilGrids in particular
   uses dg/kg for organic carbon and cg/cm3 for bulk density.

2. If a scaling factor was missed, the fix is a single division applied
   in the cleaning step. The models, the database and the website all
   stay exactly as they are.

3. Decide how to treat the above-ground pool. NPP is an annual flux, not
   a stock. Either rename it as annual carbon accumulation, or drop the
   0.47 factor if the source band is already in carbon units, or exclude
   above-ground entirely and report a soil carbon inventory.

4. If the grid extends beyond the district boundary, clip to the official
   boundary or state the area difference alongside the total.

5. Whatever you find, report the reasoning. A corrected figure with a
   documented unit audit is a stronger result than an uncorrected one.
